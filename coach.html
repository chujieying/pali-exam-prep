Best study habit

Say things out loud. Pali was a spoken and chanted language, and your memory for sound is much stronger than for spelling.

Lesson 1The Pali alphabet

Explain it simply

Pali has 41 letters (this count comes from the old grammarian Kaccāyana). They split into two teams: 8 vowels (the sounds you can sing, like a, i, u) and 33 consonants (the sounds that need your tongue, teeth or lips, like k, t, p).

Memory trick for the numbers8 + 33 = 41

“8 singers and 33 helpers make 41.”

The 8 vowels: 3 short, 3 long, 2 that swing

TypeLettersSimple idea
Shorta · i · uQuick tap, one beat
Longā · ī · ūThe bar on top means “hold it longer”, two beats
Variablee · oUsually long, but go short before a double consonant
Why are e and o “variable”?

They change length like a swing. Normally they're long: deva, loka. When a double consonant comes next, they get squeezed short: mettā, sotthi.

The 33 consonants: a trip from throat to lips

25 of them sit in a neat 5 × 5 grid. Each row is one place in your mouth, moving from the back of your throat to your lips.

Row (place)PlainPuffVoicedPlain + VoicedNasal
Guttural · throatkkhgghṅ
Palatal · roof of mouthcchjjhñ
Cerebral · tongue curled backṭṭhḍḍhṇ
Dental · teethtthddhn
Labial · lipspphbbhm
Memory trick: the 5 rows in orderKids Can Tap Their Pots

K-group, C-group, Ṭ-group, T-group, P-group. Throat → lips. Say k… c… ṭ… t… p… and feel the sound walk forward in your mouth.

Memory trick: every row has the same 5 steps

Plain → Puff → Voiced → Plain + Voiced → Nasal. Learn row 1 (k kh g gh ṅ) and the other rows follow the same rhythm.

The other 8 are ungrouped: y r l v s h ḷ ṁ

Memory trick: the 8 ungroupedYoung Rāhula Loved Visiting Sāriputta's Hut, Lastly Mmm

y · r · l · v · s · h · ḷ · ṁ. The final “Mmm” is the humming sound ṁ, called niggahīta.

Exam answer, ready to write
  • According to Kaccāyana, the Pali alphabet has 41 letters: 8 vowels and 33 consonants.
  • Vowels: short a, i, u; long ā, ī, ū; variable e, o (long by nature, short before a double consonant).
  • Consonants: 25 grouped into 5 groups by place of pronunciation (guttural k, palatal c, cerebral ṭ, dental t, labial p), plus 8 ungrouped: y, r, l, v, s, h, ḷ, ṁ.

Lesson 2What is a “case”?

Explain it simply

In English, word order tells you who does what: “The king sees the dog” vs “The dog sees the king”.

Pali doesn't care about order. Instead, the end of the word changes to show its job. Think of the king wearing a different costume for each job. The person underneath (bhūpāl-) stays the same; only the ending (the costume) changes.

There are 8 jobs (cases) × 2 numbers (one king, many kings) = 16 boxes per noun.

CaseIts jobAskExample
NominativeThe doerWho?The king walks.
AccusativeThe one something is done toWhom? What?I see the king.
InstrumentalWith / byWith whom? By what?Done by the king.
DativeTo / forTo whom? For whom?A gift for the king.
AblativeFrom / away fromFrom where?Run from the king.
GenitiveOf / 'sWhose?The king's palace.
LocativeIn / on / atWhere?Trust in the king.
VocativeCalling someone“O…!”O king!
Memory trick: the order of the 8 casesNoble Arahants Inspire Devotees And Give Loving Vibes

Nom · Acc · Ins · Dat · Abl · Gen · Loc · Voc. Always fill your charts in this order.

Lesson 3Masculine: bhūpāla (king)

This is the master pattern. Learn it well and the other two nouns are mostly copies. Stem: bhūpāl- + ending.

CaseOne kingMany kingsMemory hook
Nombhūpālobhūpālā-o = the bOss, the doer
Accbhūpālaṁbhūpāle-aṁ = what I aim at
Insbhūpālenabhūpālebhi, ehi-ena = done with Ena; bhi/hi = “by”
Datbhūpālāya, assabhūpālānaṁ-āya sounds like “for ya”
Ablbhūpālā, amhā, asmābhūpālebhi, ehiends in ā = “aaa-way” (from)
Genbhūpālassabhūpālānaṁ-assa = the king's
Locbhūpāle, asmiṁ, amhibhūpālesuends in i = “iii-n”; -esu = “surrounded by”
Vocbhūpāla, ābhūpālā“O king!” Just drop the -o
The twins rule: learn 16 boxes, remember about 10
  • Ins plural = Abl plural: both -ebhi, -ehi. “By them” and “from them” share one outfit.
  • Dat plural = Gen plural: both -ānaṁ. Think “and 'em”: for them, of them.
  • Dat singular can also be Gen singular: -assa works for both.
  • Nom plural = Voc plural: both -ā.
Ablative vs Locative: same skeleton, different last vowel smā · mhā → smiṁ · mhi

Ablative ends in ā = aaa-way (moving from). Locative ends in i = iii-n (sitting in). Same letters s-m and m-h; only the vowel at the end tells you which.

Lesson 4Feminine: bhariyā (wife)

Explain it simply

Feminine nouns end in long -ā. Bhariyā is lazy in a good way: she uses one ending, -āya, for 5 different jobs. That makes her the easiest noun on the exam.

CaseOne wifeMany wives
Nombhariyābhariyā, āyo

Memory tricks for bhariyā
  • The “āya family”: Ins, Dat, Abl, Gen and Loc singular are all -āya. Loc also gets -āyaṁ, as in “āya, I'M here”.
  • Plural Nom = Acc = Voc: -ā, -āyo. Think “all of you ladies”.
  • Calling her: bhariye! sounds like “hey!”.
  • Same twins as the king, with ā instead of e: -ābhi/-āhi (Ins = Abl plural), -ānaṁ (Dat = Gen plural), and -āsu for Loc plural (king has -esu).

Lesson 5Neuter: paṇṇa (leaf)

Explain it simply

Paṇṇa is the king's twin. It copies bhūpāla in 5 cases. Only 3 rows change: Nom, Acc and Voc. So you only need to learn 3 new rows!

CaseOne leafMany leaves
Nompaṇṇaṁpaṇṇā, āni
Accpaṇṇaṁpaṇṇe, āni
Vocpaṇṇa, āpaṇṇāni
Ins → LocSame as bhūpāla: paṇṇena, paṇṇassa, paṇṇasmā, paṇṇasmiṁ, paṇṇesu…
Memory trickN-A-V rows change

A leaf can't do anything, so it looks the same whether it's the doer or the thing done to: Nom = Acc = -aṁ. Many leaves pile up: -āni (“ā-nnni, a pile of leaves”).

Lesson 6Verbs: dhāvati (runs)

Explain it simply

A verb has a root, the core meaning: dhāv = run. The ending tells you who is doing it and when. Pali lists people in this order: 3rd (he/they) → 2nd (you) → 1st (I/we).

Present: the 6 endings everything else is built on

PersonOneManyMemory hook
3rd · he / theydhāvatidhāvantiti = that one · nti = and them all
2nd · youdhāvasidhāvathasi = I see you · tha = all of tha lot of you
1st · I / wedhāvāmidhāvāmami = me · ma = many of us
Chant it like a rhymeti-nti · si-tha · mi-ma

Say it 10 times. This little rhyme is the key to every tense.

Future: just add “issa”

PersonOneMany
3rddhāvissatidhāvissanti
2nddhāvissasidhāvissatha
1stdhāvissāmidhāvissāma
Memory trick-issa- = “I'll”

Root + issa + the same rhyme (ti-nti, si-tha, mi-ma). “Dhāv-issa-ti” = he will run. Like the present, the a lengthens to ā before mi and ma.

Past (aorist): an optional “a-” at the front

PersonOneMany
3rd · he ran / they ran(a)dhāvi, (a)dhāvī(a)dhāviṁsu, (a)dhāvuṁ
2nd · you ran(a)dhāvi, (a)dhāvo(a)dhāvittha
1st · I ran / we ran(a)dhāviṁ(a)dhāvimhā, (a)dhāvimha
Memory tricks for the past
  • “a-” = “a-go”. Put a in front to show it happened ago. It's optional, so every form has a with-a and a without-a version: adhāvi / dhāvi.
  • -iṁsu (the teacher marked this in red!): “they ran” → they're all in-su-ide now, gone.
  • -iṁ = “I'm done running.” -imha = “we did it, ha!”
  • -ittha = you all ran. It has the same tha as the present “you all” (dhāvatha).

Lesson 7Pūjā offering verses

Explain it simply

Each verse is a tiny story: what I'm offering → who I offer it to → I offer it. Learn the story in English first, then the Pali words fall into place. The highlighted words are the anchors.

1. Light · 4 lines

Ghanasārappadittenadīpena tamadhaṁsināTilokadīpaṁ sambuddhaṁpūjayāmi tamonudaṁ.

Story: camphor-bright → lamp → for the Light of the 3 worlds → I worship the darkness-remover.

Memory trick

dīpa = lamp appears twice: my dīpa (lamp) for the Ti-loka-dīpa (lamp of 3 worlds). And tama (darkness) also appears twice: tama-dhaṁsinā, tamo-nudaṁ. “My lamp for the world's lamp, removing darkness twice.”

2. Food · 4 lines

Adhivāsetu no bhantebhojanaṁ parikappitaṁAnukampaṁ upādāyapaṭiggaṇhātu-m-uttamaṁ.

Story: Please accept, Bhante → this food we prepared → out of compassion → please receive it.

Memory trickAccept · Food · Compassion · Receive

“A Few Can Receive.” Bhojanaṁ sounds like bhojan, the Hindi/Malay-Indian word for a meal.

3. Incense · 4 lines

Gandhasambhārayuttenadhūpenāhaṁ sugandhināPūjaye pūjaneyyaṁ taṁpūjābhājanamuttamaṁ.

Story: fragrant mix → incense smoke → I honour the honour-worthy → the best vessel of honour.

Memory trick

Gandha (smell) appears twice in lines 1–2. Then pūjā appears 3 times in lines 3–4: pūjaye, pūjaneyyaṁ, pūjā-bhājanaṁ. “Smell, smell, then honour, honour, honour.”

4. Flowers · 8 lines in 2 halves

Vaṇṇa-gandha-guṇopetaṁetaṁ kusumasantatiṁPūjayāmi munindassasirīpāda-saroruhe.

Half 1, the offering: these flowers with colour, scent, quality → I offer → at the Sage's lotus feet.

Pūjemi buddhaṁ kusumenanenapuññenametena ca hotu mokkhaṁPupphaṁ milāyāti yathā idaṁ mekāyo tathā yāti vināsabhāvaṁ.

Half 2, the reflection: I worship the Buddha with these flowers → by this merit may there be freedom → as this flower fades → so my body goes to destruction.

Memory trickOffer, then Reflect

Half 1 = Colour-Scent-Quality, “C S Q”. Half 2 = Merit → Freedom → Fading flower → Fading body, “the flower is me”. This verse teaches impermanence (anicca).

Lesson 8Virtues of the Triple Gem

The big secret

Each vandanā chant is the list of virtues, in order. If you can chant Iti pi so…, you already know the 9 virtues. So learn the chant, then split it up.

Buddha · 9 virtues (Iti pi so bhagavā…)

#PaliSimple meaning
1ArahaṁPure, all defilements gone
2SammāsambuddhoWoke up fully, by himself
3VijjācaraṇasampannoPerfect in knowing (vijjā) and doing (caraṇa)
4SugatoWent well (su = good, gata = gone)
5LokavidūKnows the worlds (loka = world)
6Anuttaro purisadammasārathiBest trainer of people to be tamed (sārathi = charioteer)
7Satthā devamanussānaṁTeacher of gods (deva) and humans (manussa)
8BuddhoThe Awakened One
9BhagavāThe Blessed One
Memory trick: 3 groups of 3
  • What he IS: Arahaṁ, Sammāsambuddho, Vijjācaraṇasampanno: pure, awake, perfect.
  • What he KNOWS / where he WENT: Sugato, Lokavidū, Anuttaro purisadammasārathi: went well, knows the world, trains others.
  • What we CALL him: Satthā, Buddho, Bhagavā: Teacher, Buddha, Blessed One.

Acronym: A S V · S L A · S B B, “A Sage Very Serene Leads All; Sweet Blessed Buddha.”

Svākkhāto · Sandiṭṭhiko · Akāliko · Ehipassiko · Opanayiko · Paccattaṁ. And Ehipassiko is the friendliest word in Pali: “come, see!”

Saṅgha · 9 virtues (Supaṭipanno…), grouped 4 + 4 + 1

#PaliSimple meaning
1SupaṭipannoPractises well
2UjupaṭipannoPractises straight (uju = straight)
3ÑāyapaṭipannoPractises the true way
4SāmīcipaṭipannoPractises properly

Memory trick: 4 + 4 + 1
  • First 4 all end in -paṭipanno (“practising”). Just learn the 4 fronts: Su · Uju · Ñāya · Sāmīci, “Well, Straight, True, Proper”: “We Should Try Properly.”
  • Next 4 mean “worthy of…”: Āhuneyyo, Pāhuneyyo, Dakkhiṇeyyo, Añjalikaraṇīyo. Āhun and Pāhun rhyme, so learn them as a pair.
  • Last 1: the field where merit grows. Picture a rice field.
