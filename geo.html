The big pictureThe journey of the Dhamma

Like I'm 5

Buddhism started in India. King Asoka sent teachers out like seeds on the wind. One seed landed in Sri Lanka and grew into a big strong tree. Later, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia and Laos all took cuttings from the Sri Lankan tree. And whenever one tree got sick, a neighbour sent a healthy branch back. Then India's own tree died out, but the cuttings kept it alive everywhere else.

Key dates in one line each

  • c. 528 BCE Enlightenment at Bodhgayā (age 35), 45 years of teaching
  • c. 483 BCE Parinibbāna at Kusinārā, then the First Council
  • +100 years Second Council at Vesālī, the first split
  • c. 250 BCE Third Council under Asoka, then 9 missions; Mahinda reaches Sri Lanka
  • 1057 Anawrahta conquers Thaton and brings the Tipiṭaka to Pagan
  • c. 1190 Chapata and Tāmalinda return from Sri Lanka
  • c. 1193 Nālandā destroyed: Buddhism fades in India
  • 1292 Ram Khamhaeng's inscription in Sukhothai
  • 1353 Fa Ngum founds Lan Xang (Laos)
  • 1753 Thai monks restore ordination in Sri Lanka (Siyam Nikāya)

Q1 · ★★★Why Buddhism spread so fast in the Buddha's time

Asked every year. Angles: social and religious factors · “religious revolution” · the Buddha as a teacher.

Like I'm 5

Imagine a town where only some kids are allowed in the playground, and every game costs a lot of money. Then a kind teacher opens a free playground for everyone, explains the rules in your own language, and says “don't just believe me, come and try it.” Everyone runs over. That was India 2,500 years ago.

Memory trick: 10 reasons in 3 groups “Can Monks Keep Walking? Really Rapidly, with 4 Ps!”
  • C Caste: everyone welcome (Upāli the barber, Sunīta the sweeper)
  • M Merchants & cities: rich traders (Anāthapiṇḍika gave Jetavana) carried the Dhamma along trade routes
  • K Kings: Bimbisāra gave Veḷuvana, Pasenadi became a follower
  • W Women: the Bhikkhunī Order gave women dignity (Mahāpajāpatī, Visākhā)
  • R Rituals rejected: no costly animal sacrifices (Kūṭadanta Sutta)
  • R Restless seekers: many wandering teachers; the Middle Path stood out
  • P Personality of the Buddha: compassion, wisdom, teaching to suit each person (Kisā Gotamī)
  • P Practical teaching: ehipassiko, “come and see” (Kālāma Sutta)
  • P People's language: Māgadhī, not priestly Sanskrit
  • P Preaching Saṅgha: 60 arahants sent out “for the welfare of the many”

C-M-K-W are social · R-R are religious · the 4 Ps are about the Buddha and his teaching.

Q2 · ★★★The three Buddhist Councils

Asked every year, rotating: First (2025, 2023, 2020) · Second (2024, 2021) · Third (2022). Learn all three.

Like I'm 5

Nobody had written the teachings down. So when something went wrong, the monks held a giant “say it together” meeting, like a class reciting a poem out loud so no one forgets or changes a word. That's all a council (saṅgāyanā, “chanting together”) is. They did it three times, each time because of a different troublemaker.

Memory trick: three troublemakers Rude monk → Money monks → Fake monks
  1. 1st: the rude old monk Subhadda said “now the Teacher is gone, we can do as we like!” → preserve the teaching.
  2. 2nd: the Vajjian monks took money and bent 10 rules → protect the discipline.
  3. 3rd: fake monks joined for Asoka's free food and gifts → purify the Saṅgha.

Memory trick: the numbers climb

Monks go 5 → 7 → 10 (hundreds). Months go 7 → 8 → 9. Places go R → V → P: “Really Very Precious” (Rājagaha, Vesālī, Pāṭaliputta). Patrons are “A-K-A”: Ajātasattu, Kālāsoka, Asoka (two Asokas at the end).

Picture it: Ānanda's big night

Q3 · ★★★Emperor Asoka

Asked every year. Angles: “first monarch to govern by Buddhist teachings” (2025, 2022) · “a great gift to Buddhism” (2024) · spread beyond India (2023).

Like I'm 5

A king who loved fighting won a huge war at Kalinga. Then he saw about 100,000 people dead and felt terrible. So he changed from winning with swords to winning with kindness (Dhammavijaya). He wrote kind rules on rocks and pillars all over India, built hospitals even for animals, and sent teachers to other countries, including his own son and daughter.

Before: Caṇḍāsoka (Asoka the Fierce). After: Dhammāsoka (Asoka the Righteous).

Memory trick: Asoka spells DHAMMA
  • D Dhammavijaya: conquest by righteousness after Kalinga (Rock Edict XIII)
  • H Hospitals & help: for people and animals, wells, shade trees, rest houses (Rock Edict II)
  • A Animals protected: sacrifices banned, less killing in the royal kitchen
  • M Messages in stone: rock & pillar edicts; Dhamma-mahāmattas (officers of righteousness); tolerance (Rock Edict XII)
  • M Missions: 9 missions, envoys to 5 Greek kings, Mahinda & Saṅghamittā to Sri Lanka
  • A Assembly & stūpas: sponsored the 3rd Council, 84,000 stūpas, pilgrimage to Lumbinī
Picture it: the novice who changed history

A calm young novice, Nigrodha, walks slowly past the palace window. The king is struck by how peaceful he is, calls him in, and hears the Dhamma. The Pāli chronicles say that's how Asoka's heart turned.

Q4 · ★★★Arahant Mahinda's mission to Sri Lanka

Asked every year. Angles: why Sri Lankans accepted it so quickly (2025, 2022) · his abilities (2023) · diplomacy and far-sightedness (2021) · reasons for success (2024).

Like I'm 5

Asoka sent his own son, the monk Mahinda, to Sri Lanka. Mahinda found King Devānampiya Tissa out hunting a deer on Mihintale hill, on the Poson full moon. Before teaching him, he asked a clever riddle to check the king was smart enough. The king passed, listened, and took refuge. And when the king says yes, the whole island follows.

Picture it: the mango riddle

“What is this tree?” A mango. “Are there other mangoes?” Many. “Are there trees that aren't mangoes?” Many. “Besides the other mangoes and the non-mangoes, is there another?” Yes, sir, this mango tree! Then the same riddle about the king's relatives. Smart king: lesson can begin.

Memory trick: your teacher's Five Rs (what he did)
  1. Royal acceptance: converted the king first (Cūḷahatthipadopama Sutta), 40,000 followers took refuge
  2. Reach the people: simple stories for ordinary folk (Petavatthu, Vimānavatthu, Devadūta Sutta)
  3. Royal residences: the king gave Mahāmeghavana → the Mahāvihāra; caves at Mihintale
  4. Rooting the Sāsana: a sīmā boundary, local monks (Ariṭṭha), nuns via Saṅghamittā
  5. Relics & sacred objects: the Buddha's collar-bone relic (Thūpārāma) and the Bodhi tree sapling
Memory trick: why the island said yes so fast “Friendly Language Avoids Grudges, Pleases Kings.”
  • F Friendship between Asoka and Tissa (gifts already exchanged)
  • L Language close to early Sinhala
  • A Absence of a strong rival religion (only folk beliefs)
  • G Gentle method: no force, local customs respected
  • P Personality: royal-born, wise, an arahant
  • K King's example: people follow the ruler
The line examiners love

Mahinda said Buddhism would only be truly rooted when a monk born in Sri Lanka, of Sri Lankan parents, learned the Vinaya here and recited it. That's why Ariṭṭha's Vinaya recital at Thūpārāma is the moment the Sāsana took root.

Q5 · ★★★Myanmar and King Anawrahta

Asked every year. Angles: from the early missionaries up to Anawrahta (2025, 2023, 2021) · royal patronage (2024, 2022).

Like I'm 5

Buddhism visited Myanmar a few times, like guests dropping by. It moved in for good when a strong king, Anawrahta, met a wise Mon monk, Shin Arahan. The king asked the neighbouring Mon king for the holy books. He said no. So Anawrahta conquered Thaton and carried the books home on 32 elephants.

The story in order

  • Legend Merchants Tapussa & Bhalluka bring hair relics → Shwedagon
  • 3rd C BCE Soṇa & Uttara to Suvaṇṇabhūmi (Thaton, Mon country)
  • 5th–9th C Pyu kingdom: Pāli on gold plates. Upper Myanmar has the corrupt Ari monks
  • 1044–77 Anawrahta: Shin Arahan, conquers Thaton (1057), Shwezigon
  • 1084–1112 Kyansittha: Ānanda temple, repairs Bodhgayā
  • 1180s Chapata returns from Sri Lanka → Sīhaḷa Saṅgha
  • 1472–92 Dhammazedi: Kalyāṇī sīmā, whole Saṅgha re-ordained
  • 1871 Mindon: 5th Council, Tipiṭaka on 729 marble slabs
  • 1954–56 6th Council in Yangon under U Nu
Memory trick: what Anawrahta did “Shin Arahan Taught The Super Sovereign.”
  • S Shin Arahan converted him
  • A Ari monks suppressed; Theravāda made the state religion
  • T Thaton conquered 1057: Tipiṭaka, monks, artisans, King Manuha
  • T Texts & Tooth relic replica also obtained from Sri Lanka
  • S Shwezigon pagoda begun; Pagan becomes a city of temples
  • S Sri Lanka helped: sent monks to restore ordination there (Vijayabāhu I)
Kyansittha (the “new style of building” king)

Think “Kind Successor finishes the job”: completed the Shwezigon, built the Ānanda temple with bright interiors full of Jātaka scenes that drew ordinary people in, purified the Mon Tipiṭaka against the Sri Lankan copy, kept harmony with Mon culture and Mahāyāna, and repaired Bodhgayā.

Q6 · ★★★Thailand: Sukhothai and Ayutthaya kings

Asked every year. Sukhothai (2025, 2022) · Ayutthaya (2024, 2021) · history and influence today (2023).

Like I'm 5

Thai kings acted like Buddhism's head prefects. The Sukhothai kings ordered in the “Sri Lankan recipe” of Buddhism. The Ayutthaya kings kept it going for 400 years, and one of them even sent monks to rescue Sri Lanka when it ran out of monks.

Sukhothai: think RAM and LIT

Ram Khamhaeng (c. 1279–1298) = RAM:

  • R Rock inscription of 1292: “in the water there are fish, in the fields there is rice”
  • A Araññika forest monastery + Laṅkāvaṃsa monks invited from Nakhon Si Thammarat
  • M Manangasila stone throne for monks to preach on uposatha days. (Bonus: credited with the Thai script.)

Lithai (1347–1368) = LIT: Literature (wrote the Traiphum, the “Three Worlds”), Invited the Sri Lankan-trained monk Sumana, Temporarily ordained himself.

Ayutthaya (1351–1767): “Uncle Boon Sells Bananas”
  • U U Thong (Ramathibodi I): founded Ayutthaya, Theravāda the state religion
  • B Borommatrailokkanat: ordained himself, Mahachat (Vessantara) in Thai, Wat Phra Si Sanphet
  • S Songtham: Buddha's footprint shrine at Saraburi, Tipiṭaka study
  • B Borommakot: sent Ven. Upāli to Kandy in 1753 → Siyam Nikāya in Sri Lanka

After Ayutthaya fell to the Burmese (1767): Rama I's council (1788), Rama IV founds the Dhammayuttika (1833), Rama V's Saṅgha Act (1902).

Influence on Thai life today

The king must be Buddhist · Supreme Patriarch heads the Saṅgha · young men ordain temporarily · morning alms rounds · the wat is the village centre · holidays for Visakha, Asanha and Makha Bucha · values like jai yen (cool heart) · forest masters Ajahn Mun and Ajahn Chah.

Q7 · ★★★Sri Lanka's exchanges with Thailand and Myanmar

Asked every year, alternating: Thailand (2024, 2022, 2020) and Myanmar (2023, 2021). So prepare both.

Like I'm 5

To make a new monk, you need enough monks already there, at least 5. It's like a club where members vote in new members. If a country loses all its monks to war, it can't make new ones. So it borrows monks from a friend. Years later, the friend is in trouble and the favour goes back. It's a game of ping-pong that has kept the line of monks unbroken for 1,000 years.

Q8 · ★★★Cambodia and Laos

Cambodia & Jayavarman VII (2025, 2023, 2021) · Laos & Fa Ngum (2022, 2020) · influence on society, either country (2024).

Like I'm 5

Cambodia: a very kind Buddhist king, Jayavarman VII, built 102 hospitals and a temple covered in huge smiling stone faces (the “Angkor smiles”). His son went to Sri Lanka, became a monk, and brought Sri Lankan-style Buddhism home.

Laos: a prince grew up in Cambodia and married a Buddhist princess. When he became king, she asked her father to send monks, books and a golden Buddha statue called the Phra Bang. The royal city was renamed after it: Luang Prabang.

Cambodia: Jayavarman VII (1181–c. 1218) “1-0-2 to get well, 1-2-1 to rest.”

102 hospitals and 121 rest houses. His inscription: “the suffering of the people was his own suffering.” He built Angkor Thom with the Bayon (smiling faces), Ta Prohm for his mother and Preah Khan for his father. Trick: Mother = Ta Prohm, Father = Preah Khan (“Papa Khan”).

His son Tāmalinda sailed with Chapata to Sri Lanka and brought back the Mahāvihāra lineage (c. 1190).

Cambodia after Jayavarman VII

The Chinese visitor Zhou Daguan saw yellow-robed monks (1296) · King Srindravarman left the first Pāli inscription (1309) · after Angkor fell (1431) Theravāda became the people's religion · two orders: Mohanikay and Thommayut (from Thailand, 1864) · Buddhist Institute (1930) · the Khmer Rouge (1975–79) nearly wiped out the Saṅgha · revived after 1979, now the state religion.

Laos: Fa Ngum (1353) and the kings after him

Lan Xang = “Land of a Million Elephants.” Fa Ngum's Khmer wife Keo Kengnya asked for a mission led by Mahāpāsamāna Thera, with the Tipiṭaka and the Phra Bang.

Later kings: “Very Pious Sons Shine”

  • V Visoun: Wat Visoun, made the Phra Bang the kingdom's protector
  • P Photisarath: banned spirit worship, invited monks from Chiang Mai
  • S Setthathirath: moved capital to Vientiane, built That Luang and Wat Ho Phra Keo (Emerald Buddha)
  • S Souligna Vongsa: the golden age of learning
Influence on daily life (both countries)

Laos: morning alms (tak bat) in Luang Prabang · temporary ordination · Boun That Luang, Boun Pi Mai, Boun Ok Phansa boat races · the baci ceremony · That Luang on the national emblem.

Cambodia: Pchum Ben (ancestors' festival) · Visak Bochea · Khmer New Year · temples as schools · Angkor Wat on the flag.

Q9 · ★★Decline of Buddhism, and how to keep it alive

India (2024, 2022) · Sri Lanka under colonial rule (2023) · “one country” plus preservation today (2025).

Like I'm 5

Think of a big tree. First it went rotten inside: it split into many groups, started to look just like Hinduism, and the monks lived in big rich monasteries far from ordinary people. Then a storm hit: invaders burnt Nālandā. With no strong roots (lay people) to regrow, the tree fell. The lesson: keep the roots strong.

India: Buddhism got SLIM, then hit by BPM

Inside (SLIM-D):

  • S Sects: 18 schools, then Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna
  • L Lost identity: Tantric rituals; Hindus called the Buddha an avatar of Vishnu
  • I Isolated monasteries, far from lay people
  • M Moral decline in parts of the Saṅgha
  • D Dependence on kings

Outside (BPM):

  • B Brahmanical revival: Śaṅkarācārya, Kumārila, Bhakti movement
  • P Persecution: Pushyamitra Śuṅga, Mihirakula, Śaśāṅka (cut the Bodhi tree)
  • M Muslim invasions: Bakhtiyar Khilji destroys Nālandā (c. 1193) and Vikramaśilā (c. 1203)
Any Theravāda country (2026 lesson): 4 reasons “Kings Went, Wars Came, Colonisers Conquered, Competitors Crept.”
  1. Kings went: loss of royal patronage, or hostile kings (Rājasiṃha I of Sri Lanka, Jayavarman VIII of Angkor, the Khmer Rouge)
  2. Wars came: Chola invasions of Sri Lanka (hard to find 5 monks by the 11th century), Ayutthaya sacked in 1767
  3. Colonisers conquered: Portuguese, Dutch, British in Sri Lanka; British ended Myanmar's monarchy (1885); French in Cambodia
  4. Competitors crept: Hinduism absorbed Buddhism in India; Christianity offered jobs and schooling in colonial Sri Lanka
Sri Lanka version (colonial rule and revival)

P-D-B came in alphabetical order too: Portuguese (1505, temples destroyed) → Dutch (1658, jobs only for Christians) → British (1796/1815, promised protection then withdrew it). By the 1750s no fully ordained monks were left.

Revival: Siam Nikāya (1753) · Vidyodaya (1873) & Vidyālaṅkāra (1875) colleges · Panadura debate (1873) · Colonel Olcott's schools (1880) · Buddhist flag & Vesak holiday (1885) · Anagārika Dharmapāla.

How to keep Buddhism alive today (always end with this)

Your teacher's four words: Learn, Practise, Support, Propagate.

  • Learn the original teachings and check claims against the suttas (Mahāpadesa Sutta)
  • Practise so you know for yourself and become a role model
  • Support temples, monks and Dhamma schools with time and resources: “be the Anāthapiṇḍika of your time”
  • Propagate: talks, books, websites, social media, adapting to local culture while respecting other religions

Also: keep the lay community strong, keep the Saṅgha disciplined, stay united, don't rely only on the state, do social service.

New in 2026 · short noteBuddhism in Singapore

“Influence of Buddhism in Singapore” was a 2025 short note. It can also help a “preservation today” answer.

Like I'm 5

Singapore is a port where everyone stops. Chinese, Burmese, Thai, Sri Lankan and Tibetan Buddhists each built a temple here, so all three traditions (Theravāda, Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna) live side by side. About 31% of people are Buddhist, the biggest religion.

Memory trick: two waves, one temple per country

1st wave (from the 1st century CE): traders between India, Malaya and China. 2nd wave (late 1800s): monks passing through and immigrants founding temples.

Watch out: two monks called Yasa

Yasa (the rich young man of Benares, 6th arahant) is from the Buddha's time. Yasa Kākaṇḍakaputta started the 2nd Council 100 years later. The 2025 note “Venerable Yasa” means the first one.
