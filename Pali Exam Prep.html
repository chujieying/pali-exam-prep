Part 1The Pali alphabet

Likely exam question: “Briefly explain the Pali alphabet and give its categories.”

Model answer

  1. According to the grammarian Kaccāyana, the Pali alphabet has 41 letters.
  2. They are 8 vowels (sara) + 33 consonants (byañjana).
  3. The 8 vowels are short, long or variable (table below).
  4. The 33 consonants are 25 grouped consonants in 5 groups, plus 8 ungrouped ones (table below).

The 8 vowels

TypeVowelsNote
Short (rassa)a, i, uOne beat
Long (dīgha)ā, ī, ūTwo beats, marked with a bar
Variablee, oLong by nature, but short before a double consonant

Why “variable”? e and o are normally long, as in deva, loka. Before a double consonant they are pronounced short, as in mettā, seṭṭha, sotthi. Because they can be either, they are called variable.

The 33 consonants

25 grouped (vagga) consonants, arranged by where they are spoken in the mouth:

Group12345 (nasal)
Guttural · k-groupkkhgghṅ
Palatal · c-groupcchjjhñ
Cerebral · ṭ-groupṭṭhḍḍhṇ
Dental · t-grouptthddhn
Labial · p-grouppphbbhm

Columns: 1 = unvoiced, 2 = unvoiced + h, 3 = voiced, 4 = voiced + h, 5 = nasal. 5 groups × 5 = 25.

8 ungrouped (avagga) consonants:

yrlvshḷṁ

Check the count: 25 grouped + 8 ungrouped = 33 consonants. 33 + 8 vowels = 41. ṁ is called niggahīta.

Part 3Verb conjugation

Model verb: dhāvati “runs”, root dhāv. Rows go 3rd person, then 2nd, then 1st, the usual order in Pali grammar.

Verb endings side by side

Memory hook: present endings are ti/nti, si/tha, mi/ma. The future is the same with -issa- in front. In the past, every form can take or drop a-, and the teacher marked -iṁsu as the key 3rd person plural ending.

Part 4Pūjā and Vandanā

Likely exam question: “Give the Pali formula when offering: 1) light, 2) food, 3) incense, 4) flowers.” The numbers are the line counts from the whiteboard.

1. Light · Padīpa pūjā 4 lines

Ghanasārappadittenadīpena tamadhaṁsināTilokadīpaṁ sambuddhaṁpūjayāmi tamonudaṁ.

With this lamp burning brightly, destroying darkness, I honour the Buddha, the light of the three worlds, who dispels darkness.

2. Food · Āhāra pūjā 4 lines

Adhivāsetu no bhantebhojanaṁ parikappitaṁAnukampaṁ upādāyapaṭiggaṇhātu-m-uttamaṁ.

Venerable Lord, please accept this food we have prepared. Out of compassion, please receive this excellent offering.

3. Incense · Dhūpa pūjā 4 lines

Gandhasambhārayuttenadhūpenāhaṁ sugandhināPūjaye pūjaneyyaṁ taṁpūjābhājanamuttamaṁ.

With fragrant incense made of sweet-smelling things, I honour the one worthy of honour, the highest recipient of offerings.

4. Flowers · Puppha pūjā 8 lines

Vaṇṇa-gandha-guṇopetaṁetaṁ kusumasantatiṁPūjayāmi munindassasirīpāda-saroruhe.Pūjemi buddhaṁ kusumenanenapuññenametena ca hotu mokkhaṁPupphaṁ milāyāti yathā idaṁ mekāyo tathā yāti vināsabhāvaṁ.

I offer these flowers, full of colour, scent and beauty, at the lotus feet of the Lord of Sages. I honour the Buddha with these flowers; by this merit may there be liberation. As these flowers fade, so my body moves toward its end.

Buddha Vandanā Iti pi so…

Iti pi so bhagavā arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā ti.

The 9 virtues of the Buddha (in Pali)

  1. Arahaṁ the Worthy One, free from all defilements
  2. Sammāsambuddho fully enlightened by himself
  3. Vijjācaraṇasampanno perfect in knowledge and conduct
  4. Sugato the Well-gone One
  5. Lokavidū knower of the worlds
  6. Anuttaro purisadammasārathi unsurpassed trainer of those who can be tamed
  7. Satthā devamanussānaṁ teacher of gods and humans
  8. Buddho the Awakened One
  9. Bhagavā the Blessed One

Dhamma Vandanā Svākkhāto… · 6 virtues

Svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo sandiṭṭhiko akāliko ehipassiko opanayiko paccattaṁ veditabbo viññūhī ti.

  1. Svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo well taught by the Blessed One
  2. Sandiṭṭhiko seen here and now
  3. Akāliko timeless, gives results immediately
  4. Ehipassiko inviting one to come and see
  5. Opanayiko leading onward to Nibbāna
  6. Paccattaṁ veditabbo viññūhi to be realised by the wise, each for themselves

Saṅgha Vandanā Supaṭipanno… · 9 virtues, grouped 4 + 4 + 1

Supaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ujupaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ñāyapaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, sāmīcipaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, yadidaṁ cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā, esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, āhuneyyo pāhuneyyo dakkhiṇeyyo añjalikaraṇīyo anuttaraṁ puññakkhettaṁ lokassā ti.

How they practise (4)
  1. Supaṭipanno practising well
  2. Ujupaṭipanno practising straightly
  3. Ñāyapaṭipanno practising the true way
  4. Sāmīcipaṭipanno practising properly
What they are worthy of (4)
  1. Āhuneyyo worthy of gifts
  2. Pāhuneyyo worthy of hospitality
  3. Dakkhiṇeyyo worthy of offerings
  4. Añjalikaraṇīyo worthy of respectful salutation
For the world (1)
  1. Anuttaraṁ puññakkhettaṁ lokassa the unsurpassed field of merit for the world

Sutta to learn

Karaṇīya Metta Sutta, the discourse on loving-kindness (Sutta Nipāta 1.8, Khuddakapāṭha 9). It opens with Karaṇīyam atthakusalena, yanta santaṁ padaṁ abhisamecca… Learn it from your chanting book, since the full text is not on the whiteboard.
