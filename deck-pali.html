Question 3Describe the Pāli alphabet and group the letters

Principal's cue

Pāli area 3: “alphabet.” The easiest guaranteed marks.

41 letters8 vowels + 33 consonants
Vowels (8)
  • Short: a i u
  • Long: ā ī ū e o
Grouped (25)
  • 5 groups × 5
  • by place in the mouth
The 5 places
  • Throat (k) · palate (c)
  • roof (ṭ) · teeth (t)
  • lips (p)
Ungrouped (8)
  • y r l v s h ḷ ṃ
By manner
  • Unvoiced: 1st, 2nd + s
  • Voiced: 3rd, 4th, 5th
  • Aspirated: 2nd, 4th
No own script
  • Sinhala, Burmese, Thai,
  • Khmer, Devanāgarī, Roman
Memory trick: the five groups, back to front of the mouth“K-C-Ṭ-T-P: Kids Can't Touch Tea Pots”

K throat · C palate · Ṭ roof (retroflex) · T teeth · P lips.

Memory trick: the totals“8 + 25 + 8 = 41”

8 vowels + 25 grouped consonants + 8 ungrouped = 41 letters.

Answer skeleton

Introduction

The Pāli alphabet has 41 letters: 8 vowels and 33 consonants. Pāli has no script of its own; it is written in Sinhala, Burmese, Thai, Khmer, Devanāgarī and Roman letters.

Body

  1. Vowels (8): short a, i, u; long ā, ī, ū, e, o (e and o are short before a double consonant, e.g. mettā).
  2. Grouped consonants (25), by place: throat k kh g gh ṅ · palate c ch j jh ñ · roof ṭ ṭh ḍ ḍh ṇ · teeth t th d dh n · lips p ph b bh m.
  3. Ungrouped (8): y, r, l, v, s, h, ḷ, ṃ (niggahīta).
  4. By manner: unvoiced (1st, 2nd of each group + s); voiced (3rd, 4th, 5th + y r l v h ḷ); aspirated (2nd and 4th); nasals (5th of each + ṃ).

Conclusion

The alphabet is arranged scientifically by the organs of speech and manner of pronunciation, which allows the Tipiṭaka to be recited correctly.

Question 4Translate the Karaṇīya Metta Sutta: focus on the middle verses

Principal's cue

★ “10 verses, 5 stanzas to translate; a summary also works. Not the beginning, not the end: study the middle!!!” (p. 11, thin yellow book).

Karaṇīya Metta SuttaSn 1.8 · verses 3–8 = the middle
Setting
  • Monks troubled by tree spirits
  • Mettā won the spirits over
vv. 1–2 (start)
  • Qualities: capable, upright
  • Gentle, humble, content
vv. 3–5 ★
  • “May all beings be happy”
  • Weak/strong, big/small
  • Seen/unseen, near/far, born/unborn
v. 6 ★
  • Don't deceive or despise
  • Don't wish harm in anger
vv. 7–8 ★
  • Like a mother's only child
  • Boundless love: above, below, across
vv. 9–10 (end)
  • In every posture: divine abiding
  • No rebirth in a womb
Memory trick: the middle verses' flow“Wish · Who · Where · Won't · Mother · Map”

v3 the wish (may all be happy) · v4 who (weak, strong, sizes) · v5 where (seen, unseen, far, near) · v6 what you won't do · v7 like a mother · v8 the map (above, below, across).

Memory trick: verse 4's sizes“Long, Large, Medium, Short, Small, Stout”

dīghā · mahantā · majjhimā · rassakā · aṇuka · thūlā.

Answer skeleton

Introduction

The Buddha taught this sutta to monks disturbed by tree spirits while meditating in a forest; practising loving-kindness, they won the spirits' friendship. It describes the person who practises mettā and how to spread it to all beings.

Body

  1. vv. 1–2: qualities of one seeking peace: capable, upright, easy to advise, gentle, humble, content, simple, restrained.
  2. vv. 3–5 ★: avoid even small faults; wish “may all beings be happy”, for every kind of being, seen or unseen, near or far, born or yet to be born.
  3. v. 6 ★: don't deceive or despise anyone, or wish harm in anger.
  4. vv. 7–8 ★: like a mother protecting her only child, develop boundless love for the whole world: above, below, across.
  5. vv. 9–10: keep this mindfulness in every posture (the divine abiding); with virtue and insight, one is not reborn.

The middle verses (3–8)

Verse 3 ★

Na ca khuddaṃ samācare kiñci, yena viññū pare upavadeyyuṃ; Sukhino vā khemino hontu, sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā.

Do not do the slightest thing the wise would criticise. May all beings be happy and secure; may all beings be happy at heart.

Verse 4 ★

Ye keci pāṇabhūt'atthi, tasā vā thāvarā vā anavasesā; Dīghā vā ye mahantā vā, majjhimā rassakā aṇukathūlā.

Whatever living beings there are, weak or strong, without exception; long or large, medium, short, small or bulky;

Verse 5 ★

Diṭṭhā vā ye ca adiṭṭhā, ye ca dūre vasanti avidūre; Bhūtā vā sambhavesī vā, sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā.

seen or unseen, living far or near, born or seeking birth: may all beings be happy at heart.

Verse 6 ★

Na paro paraṃ nikubbetha, nātimaññetha katthaci naṃ kañci; Byārosanā paṭighasaññā, nāññam-aññassa dukkham-iccheyya.

Let no one deceive another or despise anyone anywhere; in anger or ill will let no one wish another harm.

Verse 7 ★

Mātā yathā niyaṃ puttaṃ, āyusā ekaputtam-anurakkhe; Evam-pi sabbabhūtesu, mānasaṃ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṃ.

As a mother would protect her only child with her life, so towards all beings develop a boundless heart.

Verse 8 ★

Mettañ-ca sabbalokasmiṃ, mānasaṃ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṃ; Uddhaṃ adho ca tiriyañ-ca, asambādhaṃ averaṃ asapattaṃ.

Develop boundless loving-kindness for the whole world: above, below, across, unobstructed, without hatred or enmity.

Key words: sukhino = happy · khemino = secure · sabbe sattā = all beings · bhavantu = may they be · sukhitattā = happy at heart · tasā / thāvarā = weak / strong · diṭṭhā / adiṭṭhā = seen / unseen · dūre / avidūre = far / near · mātā = mother · puttaṃ = child · bhāvaye = should develop · aparimāṇaṃ = boundless · uddhaṃ / adho / tiriyaṃ = above / below / across.

Conclusion

Learn verses 3–8 well enough to translate any five stanzas; if stuck, write the summary of the whole sutta.

Question 5Translate sentences Pāli ↔ English

Principal's cue

Pāli area 5: “Pali ↔ English.” Some past-year sentences repeat.

Sentencesspot the ending → the meaning
Case endings
  • -ehi saddhiṃ = with
  • -mhā / -smā = from
  • -ssa = of / to
Place
  • -e / -mhi / -smiṃ = in, at
Verb forms
  • -tuṃ = to (do)
  • -tvā = having (done)
  • -nta / -māna = -ing
Tense
  • -issati = will
  • -i / -iṃsu = did
  • mā + verb = don't
Repeated ①
  • Ahaṃ ācariyo homi, tvaṃ vejjo hosi
  • = I am a teacher; you are a doctor
Repeated ②
  • Buddhamhā dhammaṃ uggaṇhitvā…
  • = Having learnt the Dhamma from the Buddha…
Memory trick: the endings“With-ehi, From-mhā, Of-ssa, In-mhi”

Instrumental = with/by · ablative = from · genitive = of · locative = in.

Memory trick: verb clues“-tuṃ to, -tvā having, -nta -ing, -iss will”

Infinitive · absolutive · present participle · future.

Answer skeleton

Introduction

Find the verb first (its ending tells person and tense), then the subject (nominative), then everything else by its case ending.

Body

  1. Ahaṃ ācariyo homi, tvaṃ vejjo hosi.: I am a teacher; you are a doctor.
  2. So pabbatamhā udentaṃ candaṃ passituṃ pāsādaṃ āruhissati.: He will climb the mansion to see the moon rising from the mountain.
  3. Having learnt the Dhamma from the Buddha, I will live righteously in the world: Buddhamhā dhammaṃ uggaṇhitvā ahaṃ loke dhammena jīvissāmi.
  4. Upāsakā samaṇehi saddhiṃ vihāre sannipatanti.: The lay devotees assemble in the monastery with the monks.
  5. The Buddha comes to the village with his disciples: Buddho sāvakehi saddhiṃ gāmaṃ āgacchati.

Conclusion

With the case endings and four verb clues, most exam sentences break down quickly. The full past-year list is on the Pali Coach page.

Answer skeleton

Introduction

Synonyms are different words with the same meaning; antonyms are opposites. Many antonyms are made with the prefix a- or an-.

Body

  1. Buddha: Bhagavā, Sugata, Tathāgata, Sammāsambuddha, Satthā, Muni, Jina, Lokanātha.
  2. Other synonyms: monk (bhikkhu, samaṇa, pabbajita) · king (rājā, bhūpāla, khattiya) · woman (itthī, vanitā, nārī) · mind (citta, mano, viññāṇa) · body (kāya, sarīra, deha) · tree (rukkha, taru, duma) · mountain (pabbata, giri, sela) · water (udaka, jala, pānīya).
  3. Antonyms: sukha–dukkha · kusala–akusala · puñña–pāpa · dhamma–adhamma · mitta–amitta · paṇḍita–bāla · jīvita–maraṇa · divā–ratti · dīgha–rassa · uṇha–sīta · ādi–anta · āgacchati–gacchati.

Conclusion

Learn the Buddha's names and a dozen opposite pairs; the a-/an- prefix gives you many more for free.

Memory trick: the four offerings“Flowers fade, Light leads, Incense invites, Food feeds”

Memory trick: the flower lesson“As this flower fades, so will my body”

Answer skeleton

Introduction

At pūjā, offerings are made with short Pāli verses that both honour the Buddha and remind us of the Dhamma.

Body

  1. Flowers: Vaṇṇa-gandha-guṇopetaṃ etaṃ kusuma-santatiṃ, pūjayāmi munindassa sirīpāda-saroruhe: these flowers full of colour and fragrance I offer at the lotus feet of the Lord of Sages. Pūjemi Buddhaṃ kusumena'nena… pupphaṃ milāyāti yathā idaṃ me, kāyo tathā yāti vināsabhāvaṃ: I honour the Buddha with these flowers; as they fade, so will my body.
  2. Light: Ghanasārappadittena dīpena tamadhaṃsinā, tilokadīpaṃ sambuddhaṃ pūjayāmi tamonudaṃ: with this light that destroys darkness I honour the Buddha, light of the three worlds.
  3. Incense: Gandha-sambhāra-yuttena dhūpenāhaṃ sugandhinā, pūjaye pūjaneyyaṃ taṃ pūjābhājanam-uttamaṃ: with fragrant incense I honour the one worthy of honour.
  4. Food: Adhivāsetu no bhante bhojanaṃ parikappitaṃ, anukampaṃ upādāya paṭiggaṇhātu-m-uttamaṃ: Venerable Sir, out of compassion please accept this food. (Water: pānīyaṃ for bhojanaṃ.)

Conclusion

Lower priority, but the meanings are easy marks if it appears. Check spellings against your chanting book.

Answer skeleton

Introduction

Each precept ends “…veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ samādiyāmi”: “I undertake the training rule to abstain from…”.

Body

  1. Pāṇātipātā: killing living beings · Adinnādānā: taking what is not given.
  2. Kāmesu micchācārā: sexual misconduct (5 precepts) · Abrahmacariyā: all sexual activity (8 precepts).
  3. Musāvādā: false speech · Surā-meraya-majja-pamādaṭṭhānā: intoxicants causing heedlessness.
  4. Vikāla-bhojanā: eating after midday.
  5. Nacca-gīta-vādita-visūkadassana-mālā-gandha-vilepana-dhāraṇa-maṇḍana-vibhūsanaṭṭhānā: dancing, singing, music, shows, garlands, perfume and cosmetics.
  6. Uccāsayana-mahāsayanā: high and luxurious seats and beds.

Conclusion

The eight precepts, kept on uposatha days and at retreats, turn the third into celibacy and add three for simplicity, training lay people in the renunciant's simple life.
